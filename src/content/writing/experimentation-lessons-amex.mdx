## The setup nobody warns you about

Running experiments sounds glamorous in theory. In practice, it's 80% alignment, 10% execution, and 10% analysis. The hard part was never the statistics — it was getting four different market teams, each with their own priorities and timezones, to agree on a test design.

UK wants to optimise for card-member acquisition. Australia wants to push premium products. Japan has regulatory constraints that change the entire funnel. And you're sitting in India trying to build a framework that serves all of them.

## The biggest surprise: nothing transfers cleanly

I came in thinking we'd find universal truths. "This targeting approach works in one market, let's roll it to all four." That happened exactly zero times.

What crushed in the UK — behavioural targeting based on browsing patterns — completely flatlined in Japan. Same product, same creative logic, completely different outcome. Cultural context isn't a footnote. It's the main text.

We learned to treat every market as a fresh problem. Same *methodology*, but never the same *assumptions*.

## The thing about digital acquisition vs. Member Get Member

Here's something interesting nobody talks about. The strategies, perspectives, even the *thinking* that works in digital acquisition through a website is completely different from what works in MGM (Member Get Member) channels. Different psychology. Different user mindset. Different conversion logic.

You can't copy your website funnel insights into a referral program and expect them to hold. They're fundamentally different acquisition systems — and treating them as "just two channels" leads to mediocre performance in both.

## Working across global teams

The most underrated skill I built at AmEx wasn't analytical. It was learning to collaborate with people whose working hours overlap with mine for exactly 3 hours a day. Learning to write a brief so clearly that someone in Sydney can execute on it without a Zoom call. Learning that "alignment" isn't a meeting — it's documentation good enough that the meeting becomes optional.

## The one thing that united everyone

Different markets, different strategies, different constraints — but one common desire. Everyone wanted to succeed. Everyone wanted to make an impact. When you find that shared motivation, the timezone gaps and cultural differences become solvable problems instead of excuses.

## What I took with me

Rigour. I can't unlearn it. Every claim needs evidence. Every experiment needs a clear hypothesis *before* you look at results. Every "insight" that doesn't survive a second look isn't an insight — it's a narrative you liked.

That discipline — of being honest with data even when it's inconvenient — is the thing I'm most grateful AmEx drilled into me. It's also the thing I see missing most often in fast-moving startups.
