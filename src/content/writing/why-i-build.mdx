## I get restless

I'll be honest — I physically cannot sit and wait for something to get built. If I can see the solution in my head and I have the skills to make it real, waiting three months for engineering bandwidth feels like watching someone drown while holding a life jacket.

This isn't some noble philosophy. It's impatience channelled into something useful.

## The moment it clicked

When I moved from American Express to Zomato, everything changed overnight. AmEx is structured, methodical, global timelines. Zomato is chaos — beautiful, productive chaos. Things move at a speed where if you can't build it yourself, the window closes before anyone else gets to it.

So I started building. A competitive intelligence dashboard because the weekly manual report was eating5 hours nobody had. A WhatsApp proxy layer because templates kept getting paused at 2am and nobody should lose sleep over that. A full content automation pipeline because I wanted to prove a concept without asking anyone for permission.

## It's not about engineering

I want to be clear — I'm not trying to be an engineer. I'm a growth person who got tired of being blocked. There's a difference.

The goal is never "build something impressive." The goal is "solve this problem before lunch so we can move on to the next one." Sometimes that means a Cloudflare Worker. Sometimes it means a Google Sheet with clever formulas. Sometimes it means a GitHub Actions workflow that replaces a whole manual process.

## The mamba mentality thing

People talk about hustle like it's a personality trait. For me it's simpler than that — I genuinely believe nothing is going to hand itself to me. Every system I've built, every result I've delivered, came from deciding "I'm going to figure this out" and then not stopping until it worked.

Not because I'm special. Because I refuse to accept "that's not my job" as a reason to let a problem persist.

## What this means if you're hiring

You're not getting someone who'll write a strategy doc and wait. You're getting someone who'll write the strategy, build the v1, test it, and come to you with results — not requests.

That's not a flex. That's just how I'm wired.
